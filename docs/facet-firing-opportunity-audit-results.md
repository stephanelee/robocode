## Why the gun holds

Of21,939cool prepared frames,21,610also have a funded aligned active plan. Only837of those submit;20,773hold. Among those holds,20,691fail only analytic drift,5fail only the power policy, and77fail both. Overlapping totals are20,768drift/82power; do not sum them as disjoint decisions. Across all cool holds, other observed failures include211without a plan,113misaligned frames and5insufficient-energy frames. These are replayed predicate results, not internally logged first rejection reasons.

The drift policy extrapolates the plan's older target state to the new scan time, computes constant-velocity future intercepts from both that extrapolated state and the new scan, then rejects if their angular separation exceeds the target's angular half-width. A small legal acceleration/deceleration or turn can change that long-horizon intercept substantially while the current target position remains consistent with the short-horizon motion evidence. This comparison also gates long/recent learned shots: sole-drift failures split12,625analytic/6,520long/1,546recent. It is distinct from the unchanged0.5degree actual-gun alignment test.

The latest target center is unchanged at(703.2291487606404,459.9476497413888), while its observed speed changes2->0 with a legal turn. The computed analytic-intercept drift is8.485375433degrees, above the2.719957981degree half-width, so this is the only failed predicate. The first actual request in that round is turn500. This establishes the observed restrictive mechanism, not that firing at30would hit or improve the outcome. Other examples and their original line numbers remain in analysis.json.

These are consecutive, dependent decision frames: firing once would change heat, energy, model chronology and later opportunities. The audit does not simulate that alternate battle, estimate additional bullet counts or attribute the failed strength screen causally to this gate. Existing source decisions already identify analytic drift as a conservative major-scan validity assumption; it is not an accidentally inverted predicate or a newly demonstrated engine-contract bug.
