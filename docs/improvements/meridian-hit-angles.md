# Meridian 0.2.0 hit-angle experiment

Meridian v1's retained Diamond Port game motivates one change. For each unambiguous confirmed incoming hit, learn the bullet's signed angular offset relative to the inferred launch bearing, normalized by maximum escape angle and oriented by lateral motion. Use those observations to distribute movement risk over plausible firing angles. Keep the v1 gun, radar, power, two routes, movement horizon and wall costs fixed. Real hits are selective labels. Exposed trajectories and genuinely safer ones can both have sparse data.

**Screen.** Four separate five-round games in fixed order. A fresh v1 control faces experimental Python Diamond Port, then the candidate faces Diamond Port, Retcon and current production AISurvivor TRAIN. Each uses a fresh process and private output paths. Compare Diamond incoming uncapped bullet damage per 100 confirmed enemy shots and official score share between the two versions. Report learning coverage (confirmed hits, unique matches, unmatched or ambiguous labels) and own shots, hits, outgoing damage, energy spent, contacts and skipped turns. Engine placements differ across games.

**Gate.** Require every registered screen game valid and zero Meridian skips. Candidate Diamond damage per 100 enemy shots must be strictly below the fresh control, and candidate Diamond share must exceed that control. All three candidate matchups must independently exceed 35% official share with at least one official round win. Otherwise reject at screen. If all pass, run three new twenty-round games per opponent. Winning requires valid games with zero own skips, mean share above 50%, at least two games above 50%, and more than 30/60 official first places separately against all three. Budget 20 screen rounds plus conditional 180. Do not replace invalid or losing games. Do not pool v1 results into this decision.

Use the v1 benchmark's pinned engine and verified loaders, including Retcon's Java 25 source launcher and Linux x86-64 Python wheels. Before competitive play publish the exact manifest, candidate and control hashes, runtime fingerprint, and independent review. Full official ticks, end states, logs and all attempts go in the evidence directory.

Engine starts remain uncontrolled. Damage rate is uncapped observer bullet damage taken divided by all confirmed opponent shots, multiplied by 100. A zero denominator invalidates the game. The observer provides official hit counts, and the private hit callbacks provide label counts. Report any difference, including terminal-event delivery.

**Implementation.** The impact event legally supplies bullet position, power and heading. Match only a single inferred launch whose projected bullet position is within one unit of the event on that turn and whose power agrees within 0.00001. Outside the fixed normalized range of ±1.25 or below one degree of lateral lead, abstain. Twenty-one signed angular bins scale with maximum escape angle. Each accepted hit adds one count and decays older counts by 0.95. Four prior counts keep v1's head-on and lead risks active during cold start. The weights preserve v1's total danger scale relative to wall and direction-switch costs. One histogram is shared across rounds in a game. Enemy misses remain unlabeled.

## Outcome

**Reject the candidate at the screen.** Meridian 0.2.0 won its five-round games against Retcon and production AISurvivor. Experimental Python Diamond Port won all five rounds against both versions. All four games and all 20 rounds were valid, with zero own skips. No confirmation games ran. Against Diamond, incoming damage per 100 enemy shots was 67.8% lower in the candidate game. Diamond's hit fraction fell from 25.2% to 12.3%, while Meridian's own gun spent 302.09 energy for 42.93 uncapped damage. Both registered comparisons with the fresh control favored the candidate. Its 7.07% score share and zero round wins failed both absolute Diamond gates.

Meridian accepted 35 of 37 confirmed hits against Diamond, 5 of 6 against Retcon and 21 of 21 against AISurvivor. The remaining three had no matching inferred launch. No match was ambiguous, unoriented or outside the accepted range. Observer and callback totals agreed in all candidate games. These observations establish that legal hits reached the histogram. They cannot separate its effect from different starting positions, power choices and subsequent interactions across games.

Within-game round shares spanned 0.0–11.5% against Diamond for the control and 0.0–11.3% for the candidate. The v2 Retcon range was 10.3–100.0%, and the AISurvivor range was 4.2–92.1%. Each arm/opponent cell has a single game, and learning persists across rounds. Between-game variability and a reproducible winning matchup remain unmeasured.
