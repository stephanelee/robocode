# Meridian v4: cheaper long-range shots

Against Diamond, v3 spent 289.09 energy firing 208 bullets and dealt 75.15 damage. Misses cost Meridian energy, and Diamond can change course while a bullet is in flight. Hypothesis: capping power at 0.4 at ranges of 300 or more conserves enough energy and reduces flight time enough to win more rounds. The cost is lower damage per hit. No opponent identification, movement, targeting or close-range change.

## Outcome: reject the power cap

**The unchanged v3 control won round 2 against Diamond Port**, dealing 71.47 bullet damage and taking 21.27 in that round. Diamond won the five-round game 480–226 on official points. The v4 candidate lost every Diamond round. It spent less per shot but did less damage, and its hit rate was slightly lower. All four games were valid with zero Meridian skipped turns. No confirmation games were warranted or run.

V3 used 355.08 firing energy to v4’s 164.07 against Diamond. The v4 firing cost fell 68%, but Diamond hit v4 78 times versus 49 hits on the control. Different starts, different opponent responses and one game per arm preclude a causal estimate for incoming damage. The control's round win establishes that v3 can win a round; neither this run nor its earlier 0/5 establishes a repeatable matchup win. Retain v3 as the experimental control. No production change.

## Reading the winning round against the port

The [pinned gun policy](https://github.com/fduch-stranger/robocode-bot/blob/67b68c22e41c18281a3fce9ef638e3c23a0b47cf/bots/ports/diamond-port/diamond_port/gun.py#L1069) explains why timing damage could matter: beyond 325 units, Diamond reduces shot power cubically once its energy falls below a threshold that depends on both bots’ energy. In control round 2, Meridian landed the first two hits (power 2.8 on turn 90 and 1.65 on turn 158). Diamond's first three hits had powers 1.95, 1.24 and 0.38. The recorded sequence is consistent with reduced return fire; it does not establish why each shot was chosen, or that landing the first hit is sufficient. Meridian also landed the first hit in rounds 3 and 4 and lost both.

Diamond's [movement planner](https://github.com/fduch-stranger/robocode-bot/blob/67b68c22e41c18281a3fce9ef638e3c23a0b47cf/bots/ports/diamond-port/diamond_port/move.py#L860) compares stopping and both orbit directions, projects its footprint as shots reach it and can consider the next shot. Its [TripHammer aiming features](https://github.com/fduch-stranger/robocode-bot/blob/67b68c22e41c18281a3fce9ef638e3c23a0b47cf/bots/ports/diamond-port/diamond_port/gun.py#L109) include our speed, acceleration, heading, wall clearance and time since speed changed. The tested cap neither forecasts those three movement choices nor changes our two-route defense. A next diagnostic would compare forecasts of stop/clockwise/counterclockwise crossings with v3's current aim at the same launches, using held-out games before spending more live rounds. This is a proposed diagnostic, not a validated strategy.
