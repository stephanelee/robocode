# Breakwater 0.2 mobile counterfire

## Why change the policy

Breakwater spent 351 of 368 Diamond shots and 679 of 687 AISurvivor shots on shielding. Just 13 and 27 shield bullets, respectively, intercepted an opponent bullet. It lost both 20-round games. Diamond took only 6.0 event damage; AISurvivor took 40.4. These observations justify testing a different use of the chassis and gun. They do not identify one cause for each loss. [Diamond evidence](../../benchmarks/breakwater-v1/README.md) · [AISurvivor evidence](../../benchmarks/breakwater-vs-ais/)

Hypothesis: continuously moving laterally and choosing among reachable paths at the arrival times of inferred enemy shots may avoid damage without consuming the gun. An independent offensive gun can then fire during evasion, learn from resolved virtual rays and exploit constrained target motion. Diamond's learned guns condition on velocity, relative heading, acceleration and walls, so a fixed orbit can become predictable. Its surfer considers multiple motion options, making a single constant-motion gun uncertain. The candidate will use seeded choices among comparably safe paths and compare offensive forecasts on common legal observations. Launch inference, incoming ray matches and virtual ray passages are estimates. Only official battles decide progress. [Pinned source analysis](../opponents/diamond-port.md)

## Frozen control policy

The mover projects at most four trajectories against the earliest two inferred incoming waves. It can keep its orbit, reverse immediately, or wait five or ten ticks before reversing. Each projected step uses the Tank Royale speed rule and moves on the old heading before turning. It aims for 430 px separation, uses a 100 px wall-smoothing stick, and seeks speed 8 while guarding the next step against wall contact. Paths are reconsidered when the selected waves change or after 16 ticks. A seeded uniform choice among options within 0.08 risk of the minimum limits deterministic tie behavior.

Danger combines head-on and linear priors (weights 1 and 2) with up to 32 recent incoming ray offsets from uniquely matched confirmed events. Normalize offsets by escape angle and by signed lateral direction at launch. Omit learned fractions when absolute launch lateral speed is below 1. Take the peak footprint-adjusted danger during the wave/body crossing and weigh the second wave by one half. The wall proximity penalty has weight 0.18. These hits are a selected sample of incoming fire.

The gun compares linear, stopped and constant-turn forecasts using the same most recent 32 resolved virtual outcomes. A cold tie favors the circular forecast. Confirmed launches create the virtual cohorts; gaps discard unresolved trajectories. Use a legal scan at most one tick old, current heading within 0.75 of the target half-width, and a cool gun. Base power is 2.0 within 320 px and 1.35 outside that range, capped by own energy/16 and by the target's finishing power, with floor 0.1. Incoming waves do not reserve the gun. Neither component reads opponent state other than legal scans and events.

The matchup IDs are `breakwater-mobile-v2-pilot`, `breakwater-mobile-v2-confirm-g01` through `g03`, and `breakwater-mobile-v2-ais-g01`. Reproduce with the tested Java 17 Gradle builds, both source/JAR parity checks, and the frozen workflow's JDK 25 command `python tools/breakwater_mobile_experiment.py --output build/breakwater-mobile-v2`. The seed controls only the corresponding bot. Official scoring determines the result. Round telemetry and the private endpoint diagnose movement, gun selection, damage, collisions and skipped turns.

The bot may not use round-end scores as a tactical input.
