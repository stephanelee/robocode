# Does recent movement help Meridian predict Diamond?

V3 learns where an opponent crossed earlier shots. It matches examples on lateral speed, radial speed, forward wall clearance and flight time. Two opponents in the same pose today could have arrived there by accelerating in opposite directions. Test whether adding lateral speed four and eight turns before the decision helps distinguish where Diamond will go next.

Freeze one training bank from the five-round v3 Diamond game in PR #81. Evaluate once on the separate five-round v3 control game in PR #83, without updating either bank. Both games have previously been studied for scores and are retrospective. Use only shots with a scan on the decision turn and four and eight turns earlier and consecutive scans bracketing the first outgoing wave crossing. Use only Meridian's incoming scans for enemy features and labels. Set context from the turn before confirmed launch. Use the confirmed bullet origin and check it matches Meridian's decision-turn pose. Preserve exclusions. Score the actual v3 bullet heading, a frozen four-feature memory, and that same memory with the two lagged lateral speeds. Both memories use every comparable training example and v3's 51-bin weighted angle vote, without its online support gate. Scale both added coordinates by four speed units. Project both historical velocities onto the decision-turn bearing and normalize lateral direction to that turn. Keep all test crossings, with training offsets limited to the v3 interval of ±1.25 escape angles.

Primary measure is the fraction of predictions within 18 units of the observed crossing center (chord distance). Report mean miss as a secondary measure. Advance to fresh testing only if sequence memory beats **both** comparators by at least two percentage points on containment and has lower mean miss than both. One split, no parameter sweeps, no new rounds. This compares bearings on existing trajectories. An alternate shot could change Diamond's behavior in play.

## Separate live test of anticipatory defense

V5 restores v3 firing power and its unchanged online gun. It estimates the enemy's earliest next launch from corrected legal scan drops and the arena gun-cooling rate. When that turn is imminent and fewer than two unpassed fronts are available, also plan against one hypothetical shot. Never use hypothetical waves as hit labels. Score continuing, reversing and stopping through the nearest incoming front, then the best of those three options through the second. Replan each turn. Hypothesis: earlier route choice and preserving an escape on the following shot can reduce incoming damage and win rounds.

Register one cold v3 control against Diamond and three cold v5 games against Diamond, Retcon and AISurvivor TRAIN, five rounds each. Require valid games with zero own skips and nonzero shots, lower incoming damage per 100 enemy creations and higher Diamond score share than the control, plus >35% share and at least one round win against each opponent. Only if all pass, three fresh 20-round games per opponent under the existing v3 majority/score confirmation rules. No reruns, no retuning after inspecting games. Use frozen v3 runtime profiles updated only for v5 assets, isolated ports and seeds, Java 17 parity and Java 25 battles. Report speculative planning and stop selections. Separately audit timing by comparing next-turn speculative warnings with true Diamond creations in official logs. A warning is a predicted opportunity to fire.

## Offline gun result

Reject the two added history features on this split. The training game supplied 206 resolved crossings from 208 launches. The separate test game supplied 282 from 286. The remaining six shots outlasted their rounds. With the 206-example bank fixed, the four current-state features predicted within 18 units on 42/282 crossings (14.89%, mean miss 105.83 units). Adding the two past lateral speeds gave 32/282 (11.35%, mean miss 107.52). The recorded v3 ray gave 22/282 (7.80%, mean miss 106.76). The historical-speed variant missed both registered criteria. The simpler memory's result motivates a separate fresh test of retaining more prior observations. It had access to a whole previous game, whereas the live v3 gun started cold. The scores measure single-turn geometric proximity on recorded paths, including shots intercepted in flight.

## Live defense result

Reject v5 at the screen. All four cold games and 20 rounds were valid with zero Meridian skips. The v3 control won one round against Diamond Port and took 28.00% of the official score. The new planner won zero rounds and took 12.08%. Its incoming damage per 100 enemy creations rose from 77.77 to 142.71. Both Diamond comparisons and the absolute Diamond gate failed. Zero of the conditional 180 rounds were played.

Against Diamond, 106 of 134 speculative warnings corresponded to an official next-turn launch (79.1% precision, 50.5% of 210 launches). Meridian considered two incoming fronts on 2,807 turns and chose to stop on 259. Of the 106 warned shots, 21 hit. Of the 104 other shots, 15 hit. Those groups occurred in different game situations. Enemy hit frequency was close across arms (17.89% vs v3 and 17.14% vs v5), but Diamond's mean power on a hit rose from 0.90 to 1.70. Anticipating launch timing alone did not solve the matchup. This screen tested the warnings, search, stopping and danger scoring together, with uncontrolled starts and one game per cell.
