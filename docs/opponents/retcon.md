# Retcon 0.2.0: pinned mechanics, limits and counterstrategy evidence

Retcon is a coherent but deliberately narrow opponent. Its pinned duel gun chooses between head-on and linear aim; its surfer evaluates two full-speed paths against one incoming wave. Those are useful attack surfaces, but they do not describe Diamond Port or Adaptive Prime, and they do not make close pressure a free win.

This analysis covers **robocode-dev/rumble-bots@28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189**, Retcon 0.2.0, MIT. All 94 selected source blobs and sizes were checked against [the immutable lock](../../tools/opponents/retcon.lock.json), using the unchanged opponent bundle retained in an actual benchmark artifact. The selected-source SHA256 is **5f7dfaaedefc39bd93d86440a2996342583d57b75b861942dc1e1899b7164bb2**. No new game or opponent modification was performed for this note. The older [Retcon strategy plan](../retcon-strategy.md) remains a historical registration; its old AIS snapshot and rejected experiments are not reinstated here.

## Energy, learning and reset behavior

Before its target-energy cap, [Retcon's power table](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/retcon/firepower/BasicFirePower.java#L19) is:

| Retcon own energy | Range below 150 | Range 150 to below 400 | Range at least 400 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| At least 35 | 3.0 | 2.0 | 1.2 |
| 15 to below 35 | 2.1 | 1.4 | 0.84 |
| Below 15 | 0.9 | 0.6 | 0.36 |

Power is capped by the target-energy inverse-damage formula; there is no hit-rate factor. [Finishing power](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/rules/Rules.java#L69) targets damage *at least* the remaining energy, rather than adding a strict-kill margin. An exact zero-energy survivor can therefore require a later minimum-power shot; the occurrence and competitive impact have not been measured here. [Fire eligibility](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/rules/Rules.java#L94) requires energy strictly greater than the requested power.

**Pressure has opposing effects.** At healthy energy, moving from range 450 to below 150 changes Retcon from power 1.2 to 3. Its bullets become slower (16.4 to 11 units/turn) and hotter, but much more damaging and aimed through a larger target angle. Short flight, ramming, walls and reduced reaction time can overwhelm a challenger too. Neither “rush it” nor “stay far” is established as a winning rule.

Retcon's pipeline is [created once per game](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/adapter/tankroyale/TankRoyaleBot.java#L81). The virtual collector [clears in-flight waves at round start](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/targeting/VirtualBullets.java#L69), not accumulated gun scores. The surfing histogram and orbit/radar state also remain in their strategy instances. Learning is in-process statistics, not a neural network or a disk-loaded model; a fresh opponent process gives a fresh game. Own-shot misses do **not** directly train its surfing histogram: only matched incoming hits do.

One reset detail merits a fixture before exploitation: [the orbit reversal cooldown](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/retcon/movement/OrbitMovement.java#L98) stores a round-relative last-reversal turn, while [the movement stage](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/stages/MovementStage.java#L19) does not reset its strategy. If a prior round left lastReversalTurn at 500, a new round's reverse request at turn 1 is suppressed until turn 510. This is a source-derived state-carryover consequence affecting orbit/fallback behavior, not proof that it occurred in a particular loss or affects the surfer's separate direction comparison.

## Physics and runtime: strengths and boundaries

The [predictor](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/motion/MovementPredictor.java#L27) implements the server's new-speed, old-heading movement order and wall constraint, while [nextSpeed](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/rules/Rules.java#L179) follows the unusual pinned stop/reversal arithmetic. Do not replace this with classic Robocode acceleration assumptions.

There is nevertheless a concrete **planner-versus-issued-intent discrepancy**. Surf simulation passes the raw Drive turn to that predictor, which limits by *new* speed; actual [arbitration](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/arbitration/Arbiter.java#L60) first limits the request by *current* speed. Source-derived example: current speed 8, reverse target −8 and mapped requested body turn 20 degrees give new speed 3. Prediction can turn 7.75 degrees, while the actual request is capped at 4 degrees. Subsequent predicted positions can diverge. This is not an observed engine failure or established cause of AIS wins; a deterministic conformance fixture and legal-scan residual measurement are needed before using it as a targeting assumption.

Likewise, wall smoothing checks a projected stick endpoint, while surfing does not explicitly penalize a simulated wall contact before returning its danger-bin score. Wall pressure is thus a plausible probe, not guaranteed trapping: its predicted speed resets on contact and it can choose the other path.

The unchanged [source launcher](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/Retcon.sh#L1) needs Java 22+ and API 1.3.1; benchmarks use JDK25 against server/runner1.4.0, with the dependency preverified by the lock. It uses preallocated bounded tables and a startup warm-up. Its one-sixth compute-budget field and optional [native export](https://github.com/robocode-dev/rumble-bots/blob/28981c7a469ac064194b41001cd7183ec5ecf189/bots/java/Retcon/src/fnl/bots/framework/stats/StatsWriter.java#L14) are diagnostic; they are not evidence about AIS or Python timing. In the three retained stronger-melee games, Retcon reported zero skips and compute maxima 9,507/11,245/11,863 microseconds. That does not justify a CPU-exhaustion hypothesis or universal deadline guarantee.

## What is measured, and what remains a hypothesis

The [fresh AIS confirmation](../improvements/final-default-confirmation-redo.md) used frozen production946/JAR9679, ten independent 20-round default-TRAIN games, pinned Retcon0.2.0, 800×600 and a 100ms turn timeout: **187/200 official first places, 86.744975% equal-game mean share, zero AIS skips**, with a game-level one-sided Student-t lower bound of 82.266939%. This supports that specific repeated duel regime, not an isolated neural-learning benefit or all-opponent strength. The [earlier invalid attempt](../improvements/final-default-confirmation.md) remains separate; the clean redo does not explain its skip.

The later [brain extraction](../improvements/replay-harness-parity-redo.md) passed its separately registered bounded noninferiority check, not a new universal equivalence proof. Against the advanced ports, the [frozen AIS baselines](../improvements/advanced-python-duels.md) still recorded Diamond 0/60 and Adaptive 3/60 first places. In [stronger four-bot melee](../improvements/strong-melee-baseline.md), Retcon itself took only 4/60 first places and 16.8855% mean share. Do not pool these populations or treat the narrow Retcon counter as an answer to Diamond.

Useful **untested** directions are feasible evasive trajectories that invalidate both simple guns, uncertainty-aware targeting over the surfer's two possible paths, and carefully budgeted wave/range pressure. Every one has a cost: reversals can cross head-on fire, private histogram state is unknown, and close pressure increases return damage. A single-wave search limitation does not prove that coincident arrivals will hit. Source inspection provides mechanisms to test, not successful strategies.

The current [separate DiamondChallenger direction](../diamond-challenger.md) takes priority. Retain the Retcon defense as a later transfer/regression constraint, leave production AIS unchanged during challenger development, and require fresh whole-AIS evidence for any transfer. Nothing in this analysis rescues the rejected scan-lag, firing-cone or learned-danger attempts, changes their gates, or authorizes a new performance run.
