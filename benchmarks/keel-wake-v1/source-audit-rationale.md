## One selected hypothesis

After a matched legal incoming-hit or bullet-collision label updates Keel's existing danger KNN, re-estimate the density of surviving waves selected for planning using their unchanged fire-time features and the current existing density estimator. Cache by hit-model sequence so each selected wave is refreshed at most once per new label epoch; do not refresh every rollout or every turn without a new label. This may reduce the delay with which early cold-game information influences decisions against already-flying bullets, improving consistency against Diamond while leaving the successful fire policy intact. It is a defensible information-timing experiment, NOT a proven bug fix or strength claim.

Specific contrast: original Keel evaluates each wave with the probability array computed once at that wave's creation. The proposed derivative evaluates it with a new array only after a later accepted incoming-bullet label changes the model, before its next use as the first or second planned wave. No epoch change, new label source, altered weights/prior/bins/KNN parameters, new movement option, power policy, gun, radar or RAM treatment. Start from ORIGINAL Keel, without Helm or Brace interventions. Keep shadows disabled.

All arrays/normalization and feature identity must remain well-defined. A refresh must not mutate learned features, alter matching, resurrect the removed label wave, or accidentally reset cross-round model learning. Round reset clears active waves but preserves hits/visits as original Keel does.

## Information legality and causal limits

A bullet's actual heading was fixed before it hit or collided; refreshing the defender's posterior cannot change that physical heading. It uses only our already-delivered own HitByBullet/BulletHitBullet events and our stored legal scans/history, which Keel already uses to train its model for later waves. Conditioning today's belief about an in-flight shot on new public-to-us evidence is legal. No assertion is made that the opponent knew our newly observed label when it fired, or that every sampled event has been learned by the opponent. Retain each wave's original fire-time features rather than substituting our current motion.

This may help if overlapping shots share persistent aim tendencies and the cold prior was poor. It may hurt if the enemy adapts rapidly: a newer-shot label can be a misleading estimate for an older already-fired bullet. Existing nearest-wave/power matching can also misassociate labels; preserve that algorithm and count matches rather than claim perfect bullet identity. If a label arrives only after all other waves passed, the treatment has no exposure. Existing aggregate hit/collision counts alone do not measure surviving-wave refresh eligibility or a final route change. No activation frequency, route improvement or cost has been measured here.
